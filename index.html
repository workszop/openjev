Why direct readout and generation disagree

Because they measure two different things, and only one of them is a probability in the strict sense.

Direct readout takes the model's logits at the first answer position, keeps only the option letter tokens, and applies a softmax across them. That is the model's actual next-token preference between the options. It reflects what the network computes, including small residual mass on unlikely options. These are conditional probabilities over the displayed options only; they are not calibrated confidence. A typical run gives something like A 0.865, B 0.000, C 0.135.

Generation asks the model to write numbers as text. The values in the JSON are just tokens the model decided to emit, not a readout of anything internal. A 0.6B model has strong habits here: it writes round, stereotyped numbers like 0.5 / 0.4 / 0.1, or collapses to 1.0 on one key, because "1.0" is a common pattern in its training data. Verbalized probabilities are known to be poorly calibrated even for large models, and for a tiny one they are closer to a formatting reflex than an estimate.

There are also two smaller contributors:

  • Different prompts. The direct path says "reply with one letter". The generation path adds instructions about JSON keys, values summing to 1 and an example distribution. The model conditions on that example, which nudges its numbers toward the example's shape.
  • Greedy decoding. Generation runs at temperature 0, so you always get the single most habitual number sequence, not a sample from a distribution.

The timing gap has a simpler cause. Direct needs one forward pass over the prompt and reads the logits. Generation needs that same pass plus one full forward pass per emitted token, run sequentially, so 30 to 40 tokens costs 2 to 3× the wall time. That ratio is what the page is designed to show. Setup, warmup and both paths are timed with performance.now(); no canned results appear.

So the honest reading of the two panels: the left one is the model's decision, the right one is the model's story about its decision.
